Schmidt Ocean Institute / experience direction

From content library to mission control.

One concept system connects orientation, live exploration, science context, and the next action without making the ocean feel smaller.

01 / evidence

Content-rich, but status can feel distributed.

The audit identified a discovery problem: visitors may need multiple clicks to understand what is active right now and where to go next.

Original audit evidencePublic-site review showed mission status, live content, and next actions distributed across multiple pathways.
Observed: strong content, split orientation.
02 / design principles

Reduce the distance between wonder and understanding.

Every screen earns its place by answering a question a curious visitor has in the moment.

1
Orient firstShow what is live, where it is, and what is happening now.
2
Translate the signalPair every technical moment with a human-readable explanation.
3
Design the next stepTurn each discovery into a path back into the story.
03 / prototype

Mission hub as the front door.

A high-signal homepage makes the current expedition a clear starting point while preserving depth for repeat visitors.

Interaction model

Next validation

Test the signal, not the polish.

Prototype with 5 visitors across three intents: live follower, educator, and first-time explorer. Measure orientation time, confidence in “what is happening now,” and whether the next action is clear.
