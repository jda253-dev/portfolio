Schmidt Ocean Institute — Website Experience Audit

Flagship design case

I audited a complex science website, mapped visitor needs and friction, then translated the findings into a clearer mission-centered product direction, prototype concepts, and a validation plan.

The problem I framed

The site had compelling raw material—live dives, expeditions, vessel technology, education, and news—but visitors could still have to work to answer three basic questions: What is happening now? Why does it matter? What should I do next?

How I approached it

I treated the public website as a product experience. I identified likely visitor groups, traced journeys across the homepage, live content, cruises, education, and application pathways, then looked for ambiguity, duplicate paths, hidden context, and weak next steps.

What I designed

I proposed a Current Mission Hub that combines active expedition context, live/latest content, location, science question, discoveries, and next actions. I also created a live-exploration module and a repeatable story structure for expedition pages.

How I would validate it

I defined a lean usability study around finding the current mission, explaining its purpose, locating a live/latest dive, and choosing a next action—then paired those tasks with measures such as completion, time, confidence, CTA engagement, and audience routing.

Why this case matters

  • Shows end-to-end UX reasoning, not only visual polish.
  • Connects audience needs to information architecture.
  • Turns findings into testable product changes.
  • Defines success before implementation.
No inflated impact claimsThis was an independent work sample. The recommendations and metrics are hypotheses to validate, not shipped business outcomes.

Yale Brain & Cognition Lab — Object Naming Study

Research + build

I helped convert an attention experiment into a participant-facing experience: instructions, trial flow, browser implementation, microphone capture, timing, and counterbalancing all had to work together without contaminating the behavior we wanted to measure.

The design problem inside the science problem

A behavioral experiment is only useful if participants understand the task and the interface does not introduce its own noise. I worked on the experience from stimulus selection through implementation and testing.

What I built and refined

I implemented the browser task in PsychoJS, including image preloading, exact trial timing, fixation and cue rendering, microphone preflight, spoken-response recording, practice and learning phases, block breaks, and experiment logging.

Why the instructions mattered

The cue is intentionally subtle: participants must mentally select one object without speaking until a later signal. The instruction screens had to explain the sequence clearly enough to reduce procedural errors while preserving the experimental manipulation.

Reliability as UX

I also treated the study design like a system that needed QA. The task automatically logs timing and generates counterbalance audits so we can catch imbalances in cue type, target position, condition, and expected responses instead of discovering them after data collection.

Interaction decisions

Case Study 03 · Computational Research → Behavioral Insight

Cornell AMP Lab — Event Segmentation in Naturalistic Video

Technical research

I worked on a research pipeline that compresses complex video information with PCA and connects changing visual structure to EEG-based models of how people segment continuous experience into events.

PCAVideo FeaturesEEG TrainingExperimental ProtocolBehavioral Science

The research problem

Naturalistic video is high-dimensional and changes continuously. To model how those changes relate to perceived event boundaries, the project needed a representation that was computationally manageable but still preserved meaningful visual structure.

My contribution

I worked within the video-preprocessing side of the project and gained code-level familiarity with PCA-based dimensionality reduction and reconstruction. I also trained in EEG experimental preparation, including participant instructions, electrode setup, and signal-quality checks.

Why this transfers to UX

The work made me comfortable reasoning from multiple kinds of evidence. People do not always articulate when attention shifts or when an experience becomes cognitively difficult; behavioral and physiological signals can reveal patterns that self-report alone misses.

Research discipline

  • Make complex input measurable.
  • Check whether the representation still preserves useful structure.
  • Understand how collection quality constrains downstream interpretation.
  • Connect computational signals back to a human behavior question.
What I did not claimThe project did not yet have final outcome data in the report. This case focuses on the pipeline, methods, and my contribution rather than inventing results.

How I work

Research is useful when it changes a decision.

My process is intentionally practical: clarify what we need to learn, choose the lightest credible method, and turn evidence into the next product move.

01

Frame the behavior

What is the user trying to do? Where might friction occur? What evidence would change our mind?

02

Prototype the learning

Build the smallest credible study, flow, or interface that exposes the behavior we care about.

03

Make the decision clearer

Separate signal from noise, synthesize what matters, and explain what should change next and why.
