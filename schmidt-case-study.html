01 / problem framing

The content was strong. Orientation was the gap.

The design problem was not a lack of compelling material. It was the distance between a visitor’s first spark of curiosity and a clear understanding of what is happening — and what to do next.

QUESTION 01

What is happening right now?

Surface the active expedition, status, location, and latest live or recorded moment immediately.

QUESTION 02

Why does this mission matter?

Pair scientific and technical detail with a short human-readable explanation of the research goal.

QUESTION 03

What should I do next?

Connect each discovery to the next useful action: watch, learn, follow, teach, apply, or share.

Design thesis

Reduce the distance between wonder and understanding.

The strongest direction was not a full visual redesign. It was a reusable interaction model that makes current mission context the front door to the rest of the site.

04 / validation

Test the signal, not the polish.

This redesign is a working hypothesis, not a claim of impact. The next step is a small moderated study focused on whether people orient faster and understand the next action more clearly.

Who I’d test with

Three visitor intents

First-time explorerCan they explain the current mission?
Live followerCan they find what is live and what comes next?
EducatorCan they move from a discovery into useful learning material?
What I’d measure

Three product signals

Orientation timeTime to identify the current mission.
ConfidenceClarity about what is happening now.
Next-action clarityWhether visitors know what to do next.
05 / current status

The product bet is now concrete enough to test.

I moved the concept beyond static screens into a coded prototype with connected home, live, case, and system states. The next step is evidence: moderated tests focused on orientation and next-action clarity.
