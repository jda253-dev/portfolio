01 / problem framing

The content was strong. Orientation was the gap.

The design problem was not a lack of compelling material. It was the distance between a visitor’s first spark of curiosity and a clear understanding of what is happening — and what to do next.

QUESTION 01

What is happening right now?

Surface the active expedition, status, location, and latest live or recorded moment immediately.

QUESTION 02

Why does this mission matter?

Pair scientific and technical detail with a short human-readable explanation of the research goal.

QUESTION 03

What should I do next?

Connect each discovery to the next useful action: watch, learn, follow, teach, apply, or share.

Design thesis

Reduce the distance between wonder and understanding.

The strongest direction was not a full visual redesign. It was a reusable interaction model that makes current mission context the front door to the rest of the site.

05 / design tradeoffs

Why this direction — and what I chose not to solve first.

Because this was a public-site concept, I did not have private analytics or direct user interviews. I treated the redesign as a focused product hypothesis and prioritized the moments most likely to reduce orientation cost.

Decision 01

Mission hub before a full navigation rebuild.

A complete IA overhaul might eventually be valuable, but it is expensive and difficult to validate quickly.

Choice: create one high-signal entry point that can prove whether clearer orientation changes behavior before touching the entire site.
Decision 02

Context beside the live stream, not behind another click.

A standalone stream maximizes visual space, but forces first-time visitors to leave the moment they need explanation.

Choice: keep status, science context, and follow-up actions in the same experience as the video.
Decision 03

A repeatable mission system instead of one beautiful page.

A bespoke layout can look impressive once, but it creates inconsistency across expeditions and increases maintenance cost.

Choice: use a small visual and interaction system that can scale across mission, live, discovery, and education states.
06 / validation

Test the signal, not the polish.

This redesign is a working hypothesis, not a claim of impact. Because this is an independent public-site concept, I did not have access to Schmidt participants or private analytics. Instead, I defined the study I would run in a resourced product setting so the concept has explicit success criteria without inventing results.

Who I’d test with

Three visitor intents

First-time explorerCan they explain the current mission?
Live followerCan they find what is live and what comes next?
EducatorCan they move from a discovery into useful learning material?
What I’d measure

Three product signals

Orientation timeTime to identify the current mission.
ConfidenceClarity about what is happening now.
Next-action clarityWhether visitors know what to do next.
07 / current status

The concept is complete at the level this project can credibly support.

I moved the work beyond static screens into a coded prototype with connected home, live, case, and system states. Since this is an independent concept without access to Schmidt users or internal analytics, I stop here rather than fabricate validation. The research plan shows how I would de-risk the direction in a real product setting.
